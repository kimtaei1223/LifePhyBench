\section{Selective-reset problem setting}
\label{sec:problem}

An evaluation lifetime $\ell$ contains $K=20$ manipulation tasks, each with at
most $H=100$ physical transitions. At each task boundary, the MuJoCo arm,
object, and goal are restored using canonical task seed 811, while scalar
thermal load $h\in[0,1]$ is retained. Only a lifetime reset restores physical
health. At the start of a lifetime, $h_0\sim\mathcal{U}(0,0.08)$.

For an $m$-dimensional requested action $a_t$, let $s_j$ be the largest absolute
bound of actuator coordinate $j$. The endogenous thermal dose is
\begin{equation}
 d_t=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}
 \left|\frac{\operatorname{clip}(a_{t,j})}{s_j}\right|^2.
 \label{eq:dose}
\end{equation}
With heat rate $\alpha=0.05$ and within-task cooling $c=0$ in all reported
experiments, the physical-step update is
\begin{align}
 \widetilde h_{t+1} &= \operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}
   \left((h_t+\alpha d_t)(1-c)\right), \\
 h_{t+1} &= \operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}
   \left(\widetilde h_{t+1}+b_t\Delta\right),
 \label{eq:thermal-step}
\end{align}
where $b_t\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p)$ is an indexed shock draw. At an
ordinary task reset, $h\leftarrow(1-\rho)h$, where $\rho$ is the
condition-specific cooling fraction. Shock and sensor draws are indexed by
lifetime, task, and step so that an early trip cannot shift later randomness.

The deployed supervisor receives one sensor value per task,
\begin{equation}
 y_k=\operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}(h_{k,0}+\epsilon_k),\qquad
 \epsilon_k\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2),
 \label{eq:sensor}
\end{equation}
not the exact $h_{k,0}$. Thermal load changes the MuJoCo actuator gains through
\begin{equation}
 \eta(h)=1-(1-0.60)h=1-0.40h,
 \label{eq:efficiency}
\end{equation}
so the gain used for the next physical transition is the nominal gain times
$\eta(h)$.

At each task boundary the supervisor commits to high power ($q=1$) or low
power ($q=0.4$). Low power scales every low-level action by 0.4. Selecting high
power when $h_{k,0}\geq0.10$ triggers protection, ends the task, and returns
$-75$. Otherwise task utility is the sum of the MuJoCo rewards after correcting
the control-cost term to the requested-action basis, plus a one-time high-power
bonus of 2. This correction prevents low power from receiving an artificial
control-cost advantage merely because its applied action is smaller.

The reported objective is mean risk-sensitive utility per task within a
lifetime. The independent statistical unit is the complete lifetime, never an
individual task. The same phenomenological law is used in both MuJoCo tasks; it
is a controlled experimental mechanism rather than a motor model in physical
units.
