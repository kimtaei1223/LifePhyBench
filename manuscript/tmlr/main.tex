\begin{abstract}
Physical state such as actuator temperature can outlive a task reset, but
ordinary episodic evaluation erases this history. We ask whether accounting for
uncertainty in such a hidden state improves lifetime decisions. In two MuJoCo
manipulation tasks, task state resets while an action-driven thermal state
persists and weakens later actuation. A belief supervisor observes a noisy
thermal signal and selects high- or low-power control from a fixed low-level
policy. Across 100 fresh Pusher lifetimes, a physics-only uncertainty margin
improved mean risk-sensitive utility by $+0.965$ reward per task (95\%
bootstrap CI $[0.733,1.196]$), although its maximum trip rate was 2.15\% and
missed a prespecified 2\% point rule. A hybrid supervisor passed at 1.3\%, with
a smaller margin effect of $+0.736$ ($[0.524,0.950]$). On Reacher, the inherited
physics-only margin again improved mean utility ($+0.721$,
$[0.474,0.978]$) but missed the rule at 2.3\%. Development-only selection of a
more conservative margin produced $+0.692$ ($[0.434,0.962]$) and 1.6\% on new
lifetimes; however, the inherited margin also passed on that sample at 1.9\%,
with no detectable mean-utility difference. The gain comes from avoiding costly
thermal trips and does not imply majority-lifetime improvement, calibration
superiority, or a safety guarantee. The practical lesson is to evaluate hidden
persistent state over complete lifetimes and to keep expected utility distinct
from finite-sample threshold decisions.
\end{abstract}
