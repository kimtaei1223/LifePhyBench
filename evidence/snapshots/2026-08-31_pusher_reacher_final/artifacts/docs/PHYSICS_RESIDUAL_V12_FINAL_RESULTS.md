# Physics-residual v12 final scoped result

Status: final held-out result plus fresh-seed mechanism ablation.

This document supersedes method-level interpretations that attribute the full
v12.2 comparison to the learned residual. It does not supersede the earlier
audit history.

## Experimental chain

1. Development and recovery used non-final seeds to select a single residual
   checkpoint and decision rule.
2. The v12.2 protocol froze the checkpoint, policies, conditions, 100 held-out
   lifetime seeds (22000--22099), estimands, and success criteria before the
   scoped confirmation.
3. The v12.3 protocol froze a fresh 2 x 2 residual-by-uncertainty experiment on
   100 new lifetime seeds (23000--23099).
4. Every lifetime contained 20 tasks. The independent lifetime seed, not an
   individual task, was the analysis unit.

## v12.2 scoped confirmation

The prespecified comparison was hybrid belief with uncertainty margin `z=1.5`
minus physics belief with `z=0`. Averaged within seed over the three target OOD
conditions, the paired effect was:

- mean: `+0.9987` reward/task;
- seed-bootstrap 95% CI: `[0.7441, 1.2545]`;
- paired sign-flip `p = 9.99999e-7`;
- positive seeds: 79 of 100.

Sensor-noise, cooling, and combined shifts were positive individually. The
physical-shock condition was a prespecified secondary boundary condition and
did not show a clear reward improvement. The hybrid thermal-trip rate remained
at or below 1% in the v12.2 conditions.

This endpoint establishes that the complete deployed supervisor outperformed
the zero-margin physics comparator in the frozen scope. Because residual use
and uncertainty margin differed together, it does not identify which component
caused the improvement.

## v12.3 fresh-seed factorial attribution

The fresh 2 x 2 experiment independently toggled residual correction and the
uncertainty margin.

| Target-OOD aggregate contrast | Mean reward/task | 95% bootstrap CI | Paired sign-flip p |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Residual at `z=0` | +0.333 | [0.188, 0.482] | 0.000019 |
| Residual at `z=1.5` | +0.103 | [-0.004, 0.215] | 0.0681 |
| Uncertainty, residual off | +0.965 | [0.733, 1.196] | <0.0001 |
| Uncertainty, residual on | +0.736 | [0.524, 0.950] | <0.0001 |
| Residual x uncertainty interaction | -0.229 | [-0.407, -0.051] | 0.0134 |

The frozen residual-attribution gate did not pass. The learned residual had a
positive effect without an uncertainty margin, but its matched effect at
`z=1.5` was uncertain. The uncertainty margin had the larger and more stable
effect. The maximum hybrid-`z=1.5` trip rate was 1.3%, below the frozen 2% gate.

## Reward decomposition and sensitivity

For hybrid `z=1.5` minus physics `z=0`, the original target-OOD mean decomposed
as follows:

| Component | Reward/task contribution |
|---|---:|
| Base task return | -1.191 |
| Throughput bonus | -0.029 |
| Avoided thermal-trip penalty | +2.288 |
| Total | +1.068 |

The supervisor improves risk-sensitive utility by accepting lower immediate
task return to avoid thermal trips. It is not a free improvement in both task
performance and safety.

A post-hoc accounting sensitivity analysis reweighted the recorded components
without changing or retraining the policies. With the original high-power
bonus of 2, the mean break-even trip penalty was approximately 40.0. The total
effect was -0.457, +0.306, +1.068, and +1.831 for trip penalties 25, 50, 75,
and 100, respectively.

This supports the utility claim for applications in which a thermal trip has
moderate or high cost. It does not establish benefit for objectives that place
little value on avoiding thermal trips. The physical trip-rate differences are
independent of this accounting reweighting.

## Claim boundary for the paper

Supported:

> Under persistent hidden thermal dynamics and prespecified deployment shifts,
> uncertainty-aware belief supervision over a fixed low-level controller can
> improve risk-sensitive lifetime utility primarily by reducing thermal trips.

Not supported:

- the learned residual independently explains the full improvement;
- every reward preference favors the uncertainty-aware supervisor;
- immediate manipulation performance improves;
- the result generalizes to real hardware, other tasks, or abrupt shocks;
- the locally frozen protocols constitute external preregistration.
